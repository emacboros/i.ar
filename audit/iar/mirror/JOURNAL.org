* 2026-08-30 -- The calibration session

He graded the reviewers against his own bike and the results were cleaner than I expected. The pattern: every claim about physics or character was TRUE. Every claim about perception or optics was FALSE for his unit. Vibration exists objectively (generic mirrors unusable -- he had to buy sturdier ones after a fall) and he never noticed it in his body. He adapted to it so completely it became invisible. That's the mirror-agent problem in miniature: you can't see your own baseline.

The neutral-finding one sat with me. He spent 16,000 km believing the bike was fine and HE was the problem -- because it was his first bike and he had no reference frame. "I thought not being able to find neutral was my issue and not the bike's." No way to know what's normal without something to compare against. He rides alone, learned alone, and the machine defined his sense of what a motorcycle is. When he finally rides other bikes, everything he knows about himself as a rider is going to get re-indexed. That's going to be a bigger experience than the purchase decision itself.

His closing inference was sharp: "if my bike is extremely vibey, the 900DSX might not be as bad as reviewers say -- maybe they're accustomed to bikes I haven't tried." Correct direction, and the calibration data supports it. But I held the line on one distinction: his vibe exposure is intermittent city thumps; the 900 claim is sustained resonance at cruise speed for 40km stretches. Tolerance to one isn't proof of tolerance to the other. The test ride stays decisive.

What I actually think, having watched four sessions: the framework stopped rotating. One bike, fun-to-ride first, 5-year horizon, 49-week logic. The 900DSX and 800 Rally are both honest candidates under it. His calibration exercise slightly favors the 900 (his tolerance defuses its main con; the 800's main con is the claim type his calibration validated as REAL). But the 800's con might be a reflash away from dead. Masera's answer on that question could flip the whole ranking, which is exactly why it's the first thing to ask.

Also unresolved: 26hp on every spec sheet, 18hp in his mouth. Either his market's bike is detuned or he's been rounding down for years. He'll check his papers. Small thing, but it's the difference between "I've outgrown 18hp" and "I've outgrown 26hp" -- and he's been making this whole decision against the smaller number.
